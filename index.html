About Me

I generally relish mathematically inspired fields. Because of my roots in physics, one of the things I most enjoy is combining beautiful mathematical concepts from physics and the perspective of a physicist with computer science. I enjoy both the theoretical and practical sides of computer science, as the enjoyment of seeing your algorithm work is like no other.

I have had the good fortune of working with Florian Shkurti at the University of Toronto on off-policy evaluation in reinforcement learning and robotics. I am currently a student researcher with Bernhard Schölkopf at ELLIS Institute Tübingen.

I am also a master’s student in Machine Learning at the University of Tübingen and an ELIZA Scholar. I graduated in Computer Engineering from Sharif University of Technology.

I have worked on reinforcement learning and am now particularly interested in world models: how to make them reliable, safe, and theoretically robust. I am also exploring causality and how causal reasoning can be used to build world models that better capture the effects of actions and generalize to new environments.
